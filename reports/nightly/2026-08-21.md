## Executive summary

Four things materially improved tonight.

1. **A crash-class performance bug is gone.** Every render stacked another click
   handler on the four persistent bottom-nav buttons. Taps replayed every stacked
   handler, each triggering another render that attached more handlers — growth
   compounded until a single tap on "בית" ran hundreds of full re-renders and the
   app stopped answering. A real Playwright click times out on the old build.

2. **A whole round now fits on a small phone.** At 320×568 the answer grid ran
   under the fixed nav: the child saw two of four pictures, and a tap aimed at the
   bottom row hit the nav and threw them out of the game.

3. **One tap is one turn.** Six handlers scored and advanced once per pat, so a
   toddler patting a picture three times burned three rounds of a five-round game.

4. **Match & Drop puzzle shipped**, production quality: pointer drag with capture
   and cancel handling, a tap→tap (and keyboard) alternative, forgiving magnetic
   hit testing that widens as a child struggles, adaptive difficulty, no game over,
   and 30 automated checks across eight viewports.

Test coverage went from one suite of 22 checks to two suites of 60, and the new
suite is the one that can see toddler problems: it uses real Playwright actions
and a DOM reachability/size audit rather than programmatic clicks.

## Bugs found

### B1 — Event-listener leak on the persistent bottom nav — **P1**
* **Severity:** P1 (app becomes unresponsive during ordinary play)
* **Reproduce:** open the app, navigate between Home and Games ~15 times, then tap
  a nav item. Measured with a patched `addEventListener`: 12 renders attached 12
  extra click handlers to `בית`; one tap then attached 15 more.
* **Root cause:** `bind()` runs after every render and queried the whole document
  (`document.querySelectorAll('[data-nav]')`), but only `#app` is torn down and
  rebuilt. `#bottomNav` lives outside `#app` and survives, so it accumulated one
  listener per render. Each tap fired all of them → N renders → N more listeners.
* **Fix:** `on()` is scoped to `#app`; the bottom nav is bound once at startup via
  a shared `navTo()` helper.
* **Regression test:** `test_no_listener_growth` (15 renders must add 0 handlers,
  plus 24 real nav taps in a row).
* **Commit:** `8b1b45c`

### B2 — Answer grid ran under the fixed bottom nav on small phones — **P1**
* **Severity:** P1 (child cannot play; the tap that misses exits the game)
* **Reproduce:** 320×568, open "איפה ה...?". Only two of the four pictures are
  visible; hit-testing the bottom row returns `DIV.bn-inner`. Same in "מה נעלם?",
  "כמה יש?" and the speech skip button; worse in landscape.
* **Root cause:** the game chrome (header banner wrapping to two rows on a narrow
  screen, stat chips, prompt box, generous answer padding) is taller than a short
  viewport, so the board overflowed behind the fixed nav.
* **Fix:** a `@media (max-height:700px)` / `(max-height:560px)` compaction layer
  for the game chrome.
* **Regression test:** `test_reachable_everywhere` — scroll each control to the
  middle of the screen, then hit-test; anything still covered is a hard failure.
  Run over 6 screens × 16 games at 8 viewports.
* **Commit:** `e1f4980`

### B3 — Controls below the toddler touch floor — **P2**
* **Severity:** P2 (frequent missed and accidental taps)
* **Reproduce:** measure every child-facing control: home/back pill 42px tall,
  "לשמוע שוב" 40px, sticker filter chips 33px, header back arrow 38×38, category
  picker 38px, gift button 40×40.
* **Fix:** 56px minimum box on primary controls; small round buttons grow an
  invisible 56px hit area via `::before` so the artwork is unchanged; a visible
  gutter between neighbouring answers so a sloppy tap does not hit the wrong one.
* **Regression test:** `test_touch_target_sizes` (≥48px on both axes, including
  any invisible pseudo-element pad).
* **Commit:** `e1f4980`

### B4 — One tap counted as several turns — **P1**
* **Severity:** P1 (a toddler patting a picture ends the game after one answer)
* **Reproduce:** in "מה נעלם?", click the correct answer 4 times inside the 900ms
  success window → score 4, and 4 of the 5 rounds gone.
* **Affected:** `מה נעלם?`, cloze, הצנצנת, two-word combinations, focused
  stimulation, the speech skip button, and repeated taps on a matched pair.
* **Root cause:** those handlers advance the round but the board stays on screen
  while the success sound plays, so the handler bound for the answered round runs
  again for every extra tap.
* **Fix:** `bind()` stamps each render with the round it belongs to; advancing
  handlers ignore anything that arrives after the round moved on. "מה נעלם?" also
  takes a per-round lock because its board is not re-rendered until the timeout.
* **Regression test:** `test_rapid_taps` — 4–5 rapid taps must produce exactly one
  point and one round in eight games.
* **Commit:** `e4f4a9a`

### B5 — The demo animation blocked the puzzle drag — **P1 (found and fixed in development)**
* Found while validating the new puzzle: the "pick me up" keyframe animation on
  the first tray piece sets `transform`, which wins over the inline `transform`
  the drag writes. The piece never left the tray, so no drop was ever detected.
* **Fix:** the drag removes the nudge class on `pointerdown`, and
  `.pz-piece.pz-dragging` sets `animation:none` as a structural guard.
* **Regression test:** covered by every drag assertion in `test_puzzle`.

### B6 — A puzzle board dealt on a large screen overflowed after a rotation — **P2**
* **Reproduce:** deal a 4- or 6-piece board at 390×844, then resize to 320×568 or
  to landscape. The bottom row of pieces sat under the nav (measured 583 vs a nav
  top of 503).
* **Root cause:** slots are square by default and the tray wraps, so a board sized
  for a tall screen is too tall for a short one. Boards are never re-dealt
  mid-play (that would throw away the child's progress), so the layout has to
  absorb it.
* **Fix:** on short viewports slots take a share of the height instead of being
  square; in landscape the shadows and the tray lay out in single rows.
* **Regression test:** the resize section of `test_puzzle` asserts every piece and
  slot stays above the nav after resizing to 320×568 and to 844×390, at two levels.

### B7 — Back closed Talki instead of stepping out of a game — **P1**
* **Severity:** P1 (navigation trap / app exit; an Android store-quality issue)
* **Reproduce:** open any game, press Android Back (or the browser back
  gesture). Talki is a single page with no history entries, so the WebView had
  nothing to go back to and the app closed.
* **Root cause:** no history integration at all.
* **Fix:** `render()` pushes one history entry when the screen actually changes
  (replaying a round pushes none), and `popstate` restores that screen with the
  round cleared — Back means "out of here", so the screen returns and the
  half-played round does not. Back from home still leaves, which is correct for
  a root screen. Capacitor's WebView goes back whenever it can and only exits
  when it cannot, so this fixes Android and the browser PWA with no new plugin.
* **Regression test:** `test_back_button`.
* **Commit:** `f7a8774`

### B8 — The flagship quiz never spoke its first question — **P1**
* **Severity:** P1 (the child cannot answer the opening round except by guessing)
* **Reproduce:** open "🎧 איפה ה...?" and spy on `speechSynthesis.speak` — nothing
  is spoken. The game asks "where is the …?" and never says the word.
* **Root cause:** only `nextQuiz()` spoke the target, and `nextQuiz` runs when an
  answer is *submitted*. The first round comes from `startGame` → `render`, which
  never spoke. `מה נעלם?`, `כמה יש?` and `לאיזו קופסה?` never spoke at all.
* **Fix:** all four now announce their round from `bind()`, the same pattern
  `receptive`/`pairs`/`sounds`/`focus` already use, with a per-round `asked` flag
  so a re-render does not queue a second utterance. "מה נעלם?" asks out loud when
  the picture disappears; "כמה יש?" says the question, which also models the word;
  "לאיזו קופסה?" names the object through `say()`, so a family recording is used
  when there is one.
* **Regression test:** `test_spoken_prompt_on_entry` — nine question games must
  speak exactly one prompt on entry, and four quiz rounds must speak four times.

### B9 — The parent gate opened once per session — **P2 (child-safety)**
* **Severity:** P2 (parent-only settings reachable by ordinary toddler tapping)
* **Reproduce:** solve the gate once, leave the parent screen, then tap "הורה" in
  the bottom nav — settings, backup/restore and reset open with no gate.
* **Root cause:** `unlocked` was set once and never cleared.
* **Fix:** leaving the parent screen re-locks it and clears the pending sum, so
  every entry is gated. Moving between parent tabs does not re-lock.
* **Regression test:** `test_parent_gate` — the gate holds on entry, a wrong
  answer keeps it shut, the right answer opens it, leaving re-locks it, tab
  switches do not, and "reset progress" still confirms and respects a cancel.

### B10 — The puzzle board did not fit an iPad or a 4-piece phone board — **P2**
* Found by extending the reachability audit with "the whole board must sit above
  the bottom nav" rather than only "nothing is covered". A column-count grid made
  slot size depend on the piece count, so a four-piece board on a phone and a
  six-piece board on an iPad mini pushed the tray under the nav.
* **Fix:** the shadows now wrap by size rather than by a column count — always the
  same comfortable square, centred on the last row — and the tray fits one row on
  a tablet. Verified: every level fits above the nav at all eight viewports.

### B11 — Silhouettes were unidentifiable — **P2 (kid comprehension)**
* A flat `brightness(0)` fill turned a duck, a penguin and a bird into the same
  black blob, which is exactly the "several nearly identical-looking items" a
  first puzzle round must not have.
* **Fix:** the shadow keeps its internal detail (grayscale and faded rather than
  filled in). It still reads as a shadow, but a two-year-old can tell a giraffe
  from a cow. `puzzlePick` also avoids two words with the same initial letter
  while alternatives exist. A residual limitation is in Remaining risks.

## Feature added — 🧩 שִׂימִי בַּמָּקוֹם (Match & Drop puzzle)

Internal id `puzzle`. Reached from the Games screen (uses the previously unused
`talki-game-card-challenge.png`, which is already Talki puzzle-piece art).

**What the child does.** Illustrated vocabulary pieces sit on a tray; their
silhouettes sit on a warm board above. Drag a piece onto its shadow — or tap the
piece and then tap the shadow — and it snaps in, the slot fills with the colour
artwork and the word appears, and Talki speaks the word.

**Language, not just dragging.** The pieces come from Talki's own vocabulary via
the existing adaptive `weightedPick`, so the board is mostly words the child can
already manage with a little challenge mixed in — never a board made only of their
hardest words. Placing a piece speaks the word through the normal `say()` path, so
a parent's recorded voice is used when one exists and Hebrew TTS otherwise, under
the existing voice/rate/sound settings. Words are marked learned and counted in the
stats the rest of the app already uses. One short spoken instruction opens each
board, as the other Talki games do.

**Difficulty.** Level 1 is two pieces, then three, four, five, six — and 5–6 only
when the screen comfortably has room (`puzzleCapacity()`), so a board is never
crowded. Picks are kept visually distinct (no two words starting with the same
letter while alternatives exist) so the first rounds are not near-identical shapes.
The level rises after a clean board, eases back after a hard one, never below two,
and persists in `settings.puzzleLevel` (a new optional key — old saves load
unchanged).

**No game over, no lives, no timer.** Failure turns into support:
* 1st miss on a piece — it floats home, soft "not there" cue, nothing else.
* 2nd miss — the correct slot lights up and Talki says the word.
* 3rd miss and after — the magnet grows (snap tolerance 0.9 → up to 2.2), so the
  board always ends up finished.

**Input.** Pointer Events with `setPointerCapture`, so a small finger can wander
off the piece without dropping the drag; `pointercancel` and `lostpointercapture`
put the piece back rather than stranding it. `touch-action:none` is set on the
pieces and slots only, never the app. Moves write `translate3d` inside one
`requestAnimationFrame` and never touch layout. Hit testing is deliberately
forgiving: the nearest empty slot within a slot-and-a-bit of the piece centre, or
any box overlap at all — the child never has to be precise.

**Accessible alternative.** Tap→tap works everywhere dragging does, and because the
pieces and slots are real `<button>`s, Tab + Enter drives the same path. Verified
playable end to end with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.

**Ending.** The finished board celebrates once — mascot, stars, restrained confetti
(only when effects are on), completion sound — and then stops and offers **עוד פעם**
and **הביתה**. It never rolls into another round on its own. Every third board adds
one quiet parent-child prompt ("אֶפְשָׁר לְהַגִּיד אֶת זֶה יַחַד?").

**Determinism for tests.** `?seed=7` (or `window.__talkiSeed`) makes word choice
and shuffling reproducible. Production never sets it, so rounds stay varied. The
parent gate's arithmetic deliberately still uses `Math.random()` — a seedable gate
would be a bypass.

## Store / privacy review

Nothing about data collection was changed tonight, and nothing was added that
collects, transmits or stores anything new. The current posture is genuinely
child-safe and the documentation matches the code:

* No accounts, no Talki server, no analytics or attribution SDK, no fingerprinting.
* AdMob runs only inside the Capacitor build (`Capacitor.isNativePlatform()`),
  initialised with `tagForChildDirectedTreatment: true`, `maxAdContentRating: 'General'`
  and `npa: true`; banner only, `BOTTOM_CENTER`, no interstitial or rewarded format.
* `AndroidManifest.xml` removes `com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID`, which
  matches `privacy.html`'s claim that the Android advertising ID is not sent.
* An AdMob failure is caught and only warns — gameplay is unaffected.
* iOS declares microphone and speech-recognition usage strings that describe the
  actual behaviour; the app is portrait-locked on both platforms.
* `privacy.html` and `store/listing.md` agree with the runtime on every point I
  checked (local-only storage, ads in store builds only, microphone on demand).

### Requires owner / privacy review

1. **The AdMob unit IDs are still Google's public samples** —
   `ca-app-pub-3940256099942544/6300978111` (banner),
   `…~3347511713` (Android app id), `…~1458002511` (iOS app id). Correct for
   development, but they must be replaced with the real units before submission.
   Not changed autonomously (production ad IDs are owner-only).
2. **Apple Kids Category advertising** — Apple restricts third-party advertising
   in the Kids Category. `store/listing.md` targets a 4+ rating and an
   Education/Kids category *and* declares a banner. Whether this specific AdMob
   configuration is acceptable there is a policy judgement, not a code question,
   and needs a human decision before submission.
3. **Play Families ad-SDK self-certification** — child-directed Play apps may only
   serve ads through a Families self-certified SDK version. `@capacitor-community/admob`
   ^8.1.0 pulls the underlying Google SDK transitively; the resolved version should
   be checked against the current Families-certified list at submission time.

Also worth an owner decision, not a policy risk: the category `<select>` sitting in
the middle of every child game screen is an adult control a toddler will tap. It
opens an OS dropdown and silently restarts the round. It was left alone tonight
because moving or removing it is a product call.

## Remaining risks / deliberately not changed

* **Offline coverage of artwork.** The service worker precaches only the 10-file
  shell; word artwork and audio are cached on first fetch. Visiting a category
  already warms it (the tile grid loads every picture), so the gap is narrower
  than it looks — it bites a child who goes offline and then opens a category
  they have never visited, or a `לאיזו קופסה?` round that pulls from a random
  category. Precaching all ~33MB is the wrong trade for a phone; a targeted
  warm-up would be the fix. Measured, scoped, not attempted.
* **Silhouettes of same-family objects.** The ghosting change makes shadows
  identifiable, and `puzzlePick` avoids repeating an initial letter, but nothing
  in the vocabulary data says two objects share a silhouette. A `shape` tag on
  the vocabulary items would let the picker guarantee visually distinct boards.
  Adding that taxonomy is a data decision, so it is a recommendation, not a
  change.
* **Landscape below the fold.** Several of the older game boards still need a
  scroll in landscape. Nothing is unreachable (asserted at every viewport), the
  puzzle fits fully, and both native shells lock to portrait, so this only
  affects the browser PWA.
* **The category `<select>` in child game screens** — an adult control a toddler
  will tap; it opens an OS dropdown and silently restarts the round. Moving it is
  a product call, so it is flagged rather than changed.
* **Speech recognition and TTS** are exercised through their failure paths only;
  no game depends on them, which the degraded-API test now proves.
* **`say()` fallback when a recording exists but fails to decode** is handled in
  code but has no automated test.
* **Native builds were not compiled.** `npx cap sync` and a Gradle build were not
  run — no Android SDK in this sandbox. The web layer is unchanged in the ways
  Capacitor cares about (no new plugin, no new permission, no manifest change),
  and the Back-button fix deliberately uses the History API precisely so no
  native change is needed.
